Kempower Satellite and ABB's A400. Here's the layout breakdown of each, and what it means for a "product page" theory.

## Kempower Satellite product page structure

1. **Hero** — full-width lifestyle photo, product name, one-line description, single CTA ("Contact us")
2. **Key Benefits** — 3-column icon + short headline + 1-sentence description (User-friendly design / Small footprint / Powerful EV charging)
3. **Product gallery/video strip** — multiple angle shots + an autoplay video showing the charger in use
4. **Quick-spec bar** — 3 large numeric callouts side by side (Up to 320kW / Up to 23ft / Plug types) — no table, just big numbers
5. **Long-form narrative sections** — H3-per-topic blocks ("Optimized Satellite Charging System," "Compact and User-Friendly," "Superior Performance") mixing marketing copy with technical facts, plus a payment-terminal sub-feature section
6. **Datasheet CTA** — "Download datasheets" banner
7. **Customer Success Stories** — card carousel of real deployments (proof/case studies)
8. **Conversion band** — "Ready to get started?" contact CTA
9. **Content cross-sell** — "Explore latest news" card carousel
10. **Software cross-sell** — ChargEye software teaser
11. **FAQ accordion**
12. **Standard footer mega-nav**

## ABB A400 product page structure

1. **Hero** — full product studio shot (isolated on white/neutral), product name + tagline, tiny inline spec strip (Output / Peak / Granularity) immediately under the headline, award badges (Red Dot, iF Gold) right in the hero
2. **Key Benefits** — 4-column icon-grid (Power that meets demand / Smart dual-charging / Brand presence / Always-on network)
3. **"Engineering Excellence" storytelling block** — alternating image + short editorial captions (UI close-ups, branding customization, anti-glare coating) — feels like an Apple-style product story, not a spec dump
4. **"Power Within" module** — 3 big stat callouts (97% efficiency / 50kW granularity / 100kW continuous) paired with an X-ray/exploded product image
5. **"Not One Size Fits All"** — modular/upgrade-path narrative
6. **Full specifications table** — tabbed (Power / Interface / System / Standards), extremely detailed engineering datasheet inline on the page (not just a PDF), plus a "Download" PDF option
7. **"Beyond Hardware"** — service/software cross-sell cards (Asset Management, Service Packages)
8. **Conversion band** — "Speak to an expert" CTA
9. **Standard footer**

## Layout theory takeaway for product pages specifically

Both follow the same underlying **"Hero → Benefits → Proof/Story → Specs → Cross-sell → Convert"** sequence, but differ in emphasis:

- **Kempower** leans more *marketing-narrative-first*, specs kept light and visual (big numbers, not tables), heavier use of real-world case studies/news to build trust
- **ABB** leans more *engineering-narrative-first*, with a genuinely awards-forward hero, an Apple-like alternating image/caption "product story" section, and a full inline tabbed spec sheet (not hidden in a PDF) — this signals confidence in technical depth as a differentiator

**Combined product-page layout theory:**
> A B2B industrial product page should open with a confident, isolated hero shot + a 3–4 item quick-spec strip embedded right under the title (not buried), followed by an icon-grid benefits section, a "story" section that alternates image and short captions to build emotional/brand connection, a bold stat-callout module for key performance metrics, a full technical specification block (tabbed if long) available inline as well as downloadable, and closing with case-study or software/service cross-sell cards before a single strong contact CTA.
